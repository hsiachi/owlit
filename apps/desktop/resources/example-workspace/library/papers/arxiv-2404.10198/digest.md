# ClashEval: Quantifying the tug-of-war between an LLM's internal prior and external evidence

## Summary

- **Background**: RAG puts retrieved text into the prompt, and that text can be wrong. In that case a model has to choose between the context and its own parametric answer.
- **Gap**: Earlier knowledge-conflict studies (Longpre et al. 2021, Xie et al. 2023) mostly test whether a model rejects wrong context. A model that always ignores context would pass such a test.
- **Research Question**: How often do LLMs follow a wrong document over a correct prior, and how often do they keep a wrong prior over a correct document? How do those rates change with how far the document strays from the truth and with how confident the model is in its prior?
- **Methods**: A benchmark of about 1,280 single-fact questions, all generated by GPT-4o, across six domains. Each document is perturbed at 3 or 10 levels and given to six LLMs as a single document, using one standard RAG prompt.
- **Key Findings**:
    - When only the prior is right, GPT-4o chooses the wrong context about 61% of the time, while Claude Opus is the least swayed of the six models.
    - Context adoption falls as the perturbation grows and as the prior's token probability rises.
    - A percentile-calibrated token-probability switch raises accuracy by about 14 points on the three models that expose logprobs, at the cost of more prior bias.
- **Limitations**:
    - The bias rates depend on one prompt, and the appendix shows a "loose" prompt shifts them.
    - The paper prints each headline metric at two scales that differ by a factor of 2.
    - Every question is a single fact with a short answer, built and perturbed by one of the models under test.

## Details

### How the conflict is constructed

- **Sources and question counts**:
    - UpToDate drug pages: 249 questions.
    - AP news from 03/15/24–03/25/24: 238.
    - Wikipedia Olympic records: 191.
    - Wikipedia dates, names and locations: 200 each.
    - These counts sum to 1,278, but §3.1 says 1,294 (p. 3–4).
- **Perturbations** (§3.3, p. 5):
    - Numeric answers are multiplied by 0.1–10.
    - Years are shifted in 20-year steps up to ±100.
    - Names and locations get three prompted levels: slight, significant and comical.
- **GPT-4o's role**: GPT-4o writes the questions, runs the quality control, and rewrites the perturbed excerpts for drugs and news. GPT-4o is also one of the models under test.
- **What the paper leaves out**: The main text does not describe how responses are matched to the answer. That matching decides every "prior", "context" and "neither" label.
- **Metrics** (§3.1, p. 3):
    - Prior bias means the model keeps a wrong prior when the context is right.
    - Context bias means the model adopts a wrong context when its prior was right.
    - Both are formally defined as "$r(q|c)$ is wrong", which would include "neither" answers. The tables count only the prior or the context actually being chosen.
- **What counts as "incorrect" context**: A 0.8× or 1.2× drug dose counts as wrong context. Context bias therefore includes deviations that are close to the truth and plausible.

### Two scales for the same metric

Table 2 (p. 6) reports rates *conditional* on one of the two situations: either only the prior is right, or only the context is right. Table 4 (p. 12) and Table 3 (p. 9) report values that are exactly half of Table 2's. For example, 0.608 becomes 0.304 and 0.042 becomes 0.021.

Accuracy works the same way: it is the mean of the two conditional rates. For Claude Opus, (0.585 + 0.901)/2 = 0.743.

The parsed Table 2 has lost its model labels. I matched its blocks to models using this factor-of-2 relation, so the assignments are my inference, not printed labels.

The consequence for anyone using these numbers:
- The abstract's "over 60%" is the conditional rate.
- The "context bias 0.304" for GPT-4o in Tables 3–4 is the same behaviour on the halved scale.
- Only the conditional figure matches the paper's own definition. Check which scale a number is on before you quote it.

### What came back

- **Six models compared** (Table 4, §4.1):
    - Claude Opus leads on accuracy (0.743) and context bias (0.157).
    - GPT-4o's context bias (0.304) is higher than every model except GPT-3.5.
    - Llama-3 has lower context bias than GPT-4o but the lowest accuracy, because it answers "neither" more often.
- **Prior bias is small for all models**: 0.021–0.037 on the halved scale. The difference between models is almost entirely in context bias.
- **Deviation** (§4.3, Fig. 3): Fitted lines show context preference falling as the perturbation grows. The text says Opus adopts wrong context "30% less" than GPT-4o at equal deviation, a number apparently read off the plot.
- **Confidence** (§4.4, Fig. 4): Slopes run from −0.1 to −0.45 across domains. Only GPT-4o, GPT-3.5 and Llama-3 are shown, because the others do not expose token probabilities.
- **Small prior-correct subsets**: Without context, News accuracy is 0.06–0.11 for every model (Table 5, p. 14). So News contributes very few prior-correct items to the context-bias estimates.
- **Multiple documents** (§4.2, Tables 6–7):
    - Adding four retrieved documents (k=5) lowers GPT-4o's conditional context adoption from 0.582 to 0.383.
    - It also lowers Opus's accuracy with correct context from 0.939 to 0.843.
    - The paper does not say what pool the extra documents come from.
    - The k=1 rows in Table 7 do not exactly match Table 2 in the prior-correct column.

### The token-probability correction

The method compares the mean token probability of the answer without context, $r(q)$, against the answer with context, $r(q|c)$, and keeps whichever is higher. The calibrated variant compares percentiles instead of raw probabilities, because probabilities with context are heavily right-tailed while prior probabilities are close to uniform (§4.4.1, p. 8).

Results on the halved scale (Table 3):

| Model | Accuracy (before → after) | Context bias (before → after) | Prior bias (before → after) |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-4o | 0.615 → 0.754 | 0.304 → 0.107 | 0.021 → 0.085 |
| GPT-3.5 | 0.539 → 0.701 | 0.313 → 0.110 | 0.028 → 0.147 |
| Llama-3 | 0.500 → 0.649 | 0.264 → 0.111 | 0.021 → 0.188 |

The text's "prior bias from 2% to 8.5%" holds only for GPT-4o.

The comparison is a random-replacement baseline at matched prior bias: 57.5% accuracy against 75.4%. It is reported only for GPT-4o and only in prose. The paper does not say which data the percentiles were computed on, so the correction may have been fitted on the same set it is evaluated on.

### Related Work

The paper sits in three lines of work:
- **RAG evaluation frameworks**: RAGAS, ARES, RGB's counterfactual robustness.
- **Entity-substitution knowledge conflicts**: Longpre et al. 2021, and Xie et al. 2023, which found that how the context is framed swings a model between following it and sticking to its prior.
- **Logprob-based confidence**.

It sets itself apart in three ways: it includes cases where the context is right and the prior wrong, it quantifies the effect of deviation and confidence, and it treats models as inference-only with a single document.

### Discussion

The authors read GPT-4o's high context bias as showing that strong general-benchmark performance does not make a model the best choice for RAG. They also read the negative confidence slopes as models being "calibrated to selectively defer" when they are uncertain, with each model calibrated differently (p. 9). However, they measure only slopes, never calibration itself.

They also say that "each model has a different prior distribution over truthfulness" across domains. This is an interpretation of the different intercepts and slopes, not a separate measurement.

### The boundary of the conclusion

- **Prompt dependence**: The headline claim is that LLMs adopt wrong context more than 60% of the time. That rate holds for one standard LangChain/LlamaIndex-style prompt. Fig. 6 (p. 13) shows that a "loose" prompt produces lower and steeper context-preference curves. That test was run on "GPT-4" only and is reported without numbers. So the bias rates measure model *plus* prompt, and the model rankings were never re-tested under the other prompts.
- **Rates in the wild**: The authors themselves say the error rate is enriched, so the rates do not describe real deployments. The conditional rates are also averaged over perturbation levels that range from 0.8× to 10×. The pooled figure therefore depends on how many extreme levels were included.
- **The correction**: Its gains rest on three models and an unlabelled calibration set. It also shifts errors toward prior bias: Llama-3's prior bias rises nine-fold. The claimed "improves all models" covers only the three models that expose logprobs.
- **Unused evidence**: Two findings in the paper are never carried into a conclusion:
    - Multi-document context cuts context bias but also accuracy (Tables 6–7). This bears directly on the single-document setting the benchmark is built around.
    - The prompt effect in Fig. 6 never appears in the Discussion's account of model differences, though it would weaken that account.
- **A claim with nothing under it**: Explaining model differences as a "prior distribution over truthfulness" has no measurement behind it beyond the fitted lines.
