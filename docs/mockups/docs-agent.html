Timing of proactive assistance

Prior work on proactive assistance has largely asked when an agent should interrupt. Iqbal and Bailey showed that deferring notifications to task boundaries reduces disruption Iqbal 2008, and later studies refined the boundary notion for mobile contexts Nguyen 2023.

To our knowledge, no work has compared task-boundary timing with user control over the suggestion itself.

Control and refusal

Chen et al. found that acceptance rose when users could defer a suggestion Chen 2025, while Sato and Ito define control as the ability to refuse outright Sato 2025. The two definitions are not interchangeable, and neither study varies timing.

Our study treats timing and control as separate factors, so that their effects on acceptance can be told apart.

Deferral Increases Acceptance of Proactive Suggestions

Chen, Park, Alvarez · CHI 2025

Abstract. Proactive assistants interrupt users at moments the system chooses. We ran a field study (N = 48) of a writing assistant that offered suggestions either immediately or with an option to defer them. Acceptance rose from 31% to 47% when deferral was available, while perceived disruption did not change.

1 Introduction. Prior work frames the problem as one of timing: find the moment of least cost, then interrupt. We argue that the cost of an interruption also depends on what the user can do with it. A suggestion that can be postponed is a different object from one that must be answered now.

We hold timing constant across conditions and vary only whether the user can defer. This lets us separate the effect of control from the effect of the moment chosen.

2 Related work. Notification research established that interruptions at task boundaries are less disruptive. Work on mixed-initiative interfaces argued that users should be able to direct and dismiss automated services.
